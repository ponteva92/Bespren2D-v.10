# Bespren — Game Design Document & Technical Direction

**Status:** Mobile LAN world-map, salvage, auto-aim, and defense-combat vertical slice, Godot 4.7.1 Mobile  
**Target:** Android, 480×270 logical landscape viewport, local two-player co-op  
**Art direction:** AAA+ post-apocalyptic survival readability, authored for constrained mobile rendering  
**Document role:** Living product contract for design, engineering, art, and validation

## 1. Product vision

Bespren is a top-down survival action RPG about two scavengers holding a fragile technological refuge together after infrastructure and ecology have collapsed. Its emotional center is the Base Core: a warm, rhythmic signal in a world whose rust, rot, rain, and oxidized machinery constantly communicate loss. The game should make cooperation feel physical and legible even on a small shared screen, with Heikki and Shane reading as complementary silhouettes before the player notices individual equipment.

The long-term thirty-second loop is **scan → salvage → return → reinforce → survive**. The current vertical slice supplies a character-select boot menu, safe-area-aware multitouch movement and action triggers, a 28,672-unit multi-biome world, host-resolved static collisions, deterministic three-to-five-step host-authoritative resource gathering with unchanged one-unit node yield, a replicated shared resource pool, the 450-second day/night clock and zombie horde, eight Tier-1 construction cards, three automatic towers, three functional traps, 64px 2D placement, transactional Base relocation, weighted zombie auto-aim, visible host-approved projectiles, eight distinct enemy presentations, pooled action/UI audio, ENet transport, automatic Solo fallback, and validation harness. Base deposit, persistent inventory, long-term progression, and device certification remain outside the implemented scope.

## 2. Design pillars

**The refuge is a character.** The Base Core is always warmer, rounder, and more rhythmic than the world around it. Its Amber Gold PointLight2D and aura establish safety without requiring dialogue, while any future state change must remain readable through pulse cadence, value, and shape rather than color alone.

**Two players, two signatures.** Heikki owns a broad, stable silhouette with Gold utility accents; Shane owns a narrower, asymmetric silhouette with Cyan/Teal technology accents. Local co-op must preserve independent input ownership and never infer that two actions from one device represent two players.

**Decay tells history.** Rust is granular and hot, rotten wood has directional grain and dark cavities, and oxidized copper blooms in cool organic islands. Weathering may enrich surfaces, but silhouettes, interaction edges, and navigation-critical values must remain cleaner than decorative background regions.

**Mobile clarity is the quality bar.** “AAA+” here means coherent art direction, disciplined hierarchy, reproducible assets, controlled effects, and strong validation—not desktop-only post-processing or excessive texture resolution. Every visual choice must survive the 480×270 logical target, mid-range Android bandwidth limits, touch occlusion, and sustained thermal load.

## 3. Audience, format, and session promise

The intended audience values compact cooperative survival sessions, visible base growth, scavenging tension, and expressive character roles. The current playable format is one local touch player per Android device with Solo, Host LAN, and Join LAN launch paths; keyboard input remains available for desktop development. The earlier two-seat shared-screen roster remains a validated data contract, but the shipping runtime slice now exercises peer-one authority through either ENet on UDP 8791 or `OfflineMultiplayerPeer` fallback.

Sessions should ultimately support short ten-minute salvage runs and longer fortification arcs without requiring an always-on connection. `OfflineMultiplayerPeer` makes the local SceneTree authoritative at peer ID 1, and the ENet runtime preserves that same authority boundary on UDP 8791. Two-process desktop loopback probes now cover movement, actions, host-selected resource gathering, replicated depletion, and replicated inventory; physical two-device Android LAN behavior remains a separate release gate.

## 4. Core loop and progression contract

The implemented moment-to-moment slice lets players read the world, move between regions, identify Wood, Metal, and Tech, and gather through the host-validated `[USE]` path. Each node accepts a deterministic three-to-five interactions, grants nothing on intermediate steps, and grants the same single resource unit only on completion; replicated progress and late-join snapshots preserve that partial work. Gather commits feed the shared construction pool. During the 450-second day, players may inspect and place any affordable Tier-1 structure; night begins automatically or through a host-validated command. Kinetic, Chemical, and Electric towers acquire zombies only inside their configured ranges and apply direct, area, or chain damage. Landmine, Slowing Pit, and Razor Snare implement single-use blast, persistent slow, and reusable damage/root behavior respectively. Traps remain placeable structures but do not block player motion or flow-field navigation.

Long-term progression should combine player mastery, persistent refuge upgrades, character-specific tools, and access to new regions. Resource colors are already reserved as semantic anchors: Wood uses `#FFD700`, Metal uses `#E0E0E0`, and Tech uses `#00FFFF`. Future UI, loot feedback, and upgrade previews must reuse these anchors while adding icons, patterns, and labels for color-vision accessibility.

## 5. Technical architecture

The application boots through `res://scenes/ui/StartMenu.tscn`; it selects Heikki or Shane and configures `res://scenes/game/game_world.tscn` before that scene enters the tree. `GameWorld` composes `CoopSession`, `DayNightCycle`, `TacticalBuildSystem`, `GridPlacementController2D`, `BesprenWorldMap2D`, `BesprenResourceScatter2D`, the Y-sorted player/projectile/defense roots, Base Core presentation, `JuiceRig`, `GameAudioController`, `GameHUD`, and `MobileControls`, while the earlier `bespren_foundation.tscn` remains the visual/local-roster validation composition. Gameplay domains must continue to arrive as composed child systems or typed Resources rather than being folded into the menu or bootstrap.

The architecture uses explicit Godot types, `class_name`, typed arrays, typed signals, enums, node-path contracts, and Resource-backed seat definitions. Cross-domain behavior should communicate through narrow signals or validated commands; nodes must not reach through unrelated scene branches. Autoloads are intentionally absent because no system in this milestone requires process-global lifetime.

```text
res://
├── assets/2d/                 # Curated runtime art only
│   ├── actors/                # Baked 8-direction actor sheets, SpriteFrames, scenes
│   ├── characters/            # Blender-rendered Heikki and Shane sprites
│   ├── effects/               # Base Core and radial light sources
│   ├── environment/           # Offline-baked collidable environment sprites
│   └── resources/             # Wood, Metal, Tech vector masters
├── data/coop/                 # Reserved for authored seat/profile Resources
├── scenes/
│   ├── characters/            # Presentation-only character scenes
│   ├── main/                  # Composition root
│   ├── resources/             # Presentation-only resource scenes
│   ├── showcase/              # Integrated art-direction target
│   └── world/                 # Base Core visual scene
├── shaders/                   # Mobile CanvasItem shaders
├── src/
│   ├── coop/                  # Seat ownership and authority boundary
│   ├── core/                  # Application composition

```

The root-level `Addons/` directory is an external source-asset vault and contains many independent projects and third-party packages. `Addons/.gdignore` prevents Godot from treating that archive as production `res://` content, while no source asset has been deleted. `tools/asset_pipeline/audit_and_extract_runtime_assets.py` recursively inventories the vault, fully decompresses ZIP members for path/CRC/safety validation, promotes only reviewed runtime art/audio and license files, verifies copied hashes, and writes `assets/runtime_asset_manifest.json`. Any later promotion must preserve that provenance and scene-level reachability.

## 6. Local co-op and authority contract

`PlayerSlotDefinition` describes a seat using an ID, display name, control scheme, device ID, and identity color. `LocalCoopRoster` owns at most two unique seats, rejects duplicate joypad ownership, routes physical events to a seat, and returns defensive copies of its typed array. The default contract is Heikki on touch/keyboard and Shane on joypad device 1.

`CoopSession` starts Solo, ENet Host, or ENet Client sessions on UDP 8791. Replaceable movement requests and snapshots use `unreliable_ordered`; registration and Interact/Fire commits remain `reliable`. Clients address peer 1 with `rpc_id(1)`, the host validates character IDs, registered peers, finite unit movement vectors, interaction cooldowns, and the two allowed action identifiers, then integrates and rebroadcasts authoritative `Vector2` state at 20 Hz. Accepted fire commits include the host-recorded facing direction so every peer renders the same projectile. A failed or disconnected client automatically replaces its peer with `OfflineMultiplayerPeer`, preserving the exact same peer-one authority path.

`AutoAimController2D` derives candidates from the bounded host-authoritative horde registry and an `Area2D`, then scores distance, aim angle, threat, and continuity instead of choosing nearest-only. A 150-degree cone, line-of-sight invalidation, lock hysteresis, minimum lock time, and smoothed presentation prevent target flicker while keeping the server's final fire direction authoritative. Invalid or occluded locks are synchronously rejected again at fire resolution.

`MobileControls` owns independent finger indices for movement, Interact, and Fire, consumes every owned UI touch before unhandled world input, quantizes the left stick to eight directions, and exposes two 44×44 logical action targets. Safe-area insets are converted from display coordinates into the 480×270 logical canvas before control placement. A second local touch player must not be added by reusing global `Input` polling; it requires another explicit seat-scoped provider and conflict map.

Godot’s `InputEvent.device` property provides the physical device identifier used by the current router, and Godot supports multiple simultaneous controllers. The offline peer contract intentionally mirrors a server with no connected peers, for which `is_server()` is true and the unique ID is the server target. These choices follow the official [InputEvent](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_inputevent.html), [controller](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/inputs/controllers_gamepads_joysticks.html), and [OfflineMultiplayerPeer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_offlinemultiplayerpeer.html) documentation.

## 7. Scene contracts

`base_core.tscn` is a presentation scene containing a Blender-authored survivor camp: canvas tent, fire pit, workbench, crates, radio mast, and sandbag perimeter. It retains an Amber Gold `PointLight2D`, restrained radial aura, additive scale-glow silhouette, and typed pulse driver. The driver varies presentation only and owns the exact 1.15× command scale-punch; it owns no health, build radius, inventory, or defensive authority. Future Base mechanics must compose beside this view rather than turning the visual driver into a game-state authority.

The Wood, Metal, and Tech scenes each contain an SVG icon, a dynamically pulsing toon/emissive shader material, a visibility-gated identity light, and absolute gameplay-pass Z. `ResourceNodeView` selects identity and presents authoritative partial-harvest feedback through bounded scale, alpha, glow, and hit response; it still owns no quantity, regeneration, ownership, or pickup decision. `BesprenResourceScatter2D` owns deterministic IDs, three-to-five-step durability, per-peer focus locks, range validation, totals, ordered progress revisions, final depletion, and late-join snapshots.

Heikki and Shane scenes use Blender-rendered RGBA sprites with a shared toon-outline shader and separate accent lights. `CharacterPresentation` duplicates its material before applying the accent, preventing one instance from mutating another through a shared `ShaderMaterial`. `network_player.tscn` wraps either presentation in a radius-9 `CharacterBody2D` on Players/WorldStatic collision filters, follows authoritative `Vector2` state through `move_and_slide()`, and switches its `AnimationPlayer` between looping Idle and Run clips from actual velocity. Equipment, abilities, and damage state remain absent.

`GameHUD` owns the `148 x 58` layer-100 horizontal resource/action dashboard, `72 x 54` interactive minimap, independent wave chip, transient safe-area toast, and eight-card categorized deck without deciding authoritative outcomes. Each resource reads as a recessed `46 x 12` plate carrying a 12px icon and a right aligned count, and each command as a raised `40 x 29` plate whose pressed state is derived from the raised one in script rather than authored beside it. Only the slot that actually rose is popped on a commit, so the beat says which resource arrived instead of merely that something did. `TacticalBuildSystem` owns the shared pool, host validation, structure commits, tower/trap runtime snapshots, placement overlap, blocking-structure collision resolution, and Base relocation transaction. `PlacedStructure2D` gives every catalog ID a distinct Blender-rendered top-down silhouette; towers and utilities may own `WorldStatic` footprints while all three ground traps remain nonblocking. `GameAudioController` extends `AudioManager`, preserving six bounded `AudioStreamPlayer2D` world voices while adding an eight-voice mono pool for pitch-varied UI thuds and alerts. `PlayerProjectile` renders a high-contrast tracer and imported projectile head, performs continuous WorldStatic/Enemies ray collision, applies host-authorized enemy damage, and always frees itself on impact or timeout.

That rest treatment reached the fill and left the loudest element of the readout untouched, and the mechanism is worth more than the fix. The bevel is one lifted row along the top of the fill - at three screen pixels tall a gradient is invisible, so a single bright band is the whole thing that stops the bar reading as flat vector art - and it was drawn as `fill.lightened(0.42)`. `Color.lightened` is affine toward white rather than a multiplier: it maps 0 to 0.42 and 1 to 1, so it is compressive, and applying it after a darkening re-inflates exactly what the darkening removed. `REST_DARKEN`'s 0.45 multiplier on the fill therefore arrived as 0.72 on the bevel. At rest the band sat at 1.844x its own fill authored and 1.888x delivered, against the 1.149x every other state the bar can be in ships; measured on the camp capture it ran at mean luma 210.7 in a frame whose mean is 52.0, and it held 182 of the 1,113 frame pixels above that value - 16.4 percent of the frame's brightest population, on a widget occupying 0.33 percent of the frame. **A compressive operator applied after a treatment undoes the treatment.** The fix is an order reversal in the resting branch alone - the band is lifted first and the result darkened, so the lift survives as a ratio instead of being re-inflated by it - plus the promotion of the magic 0.42 to `BEVEL_LIGHTEN` beside its sibling `CHIP_LIGHTEN`. Every other state routes through `fill_color().lightened(BEVEL_LIGHTEN)` and is byte-identical to before, and the chip needs no change at all, because a chip only exists while `_chip > _ratio` and that is never a resting state.

A band chosen to summarise a silhouette is itself the measurement: the bottom-decile sliver and the bottom-quartile band disagreed by a factor of two about the same shadow, and the sliver produced a confident 1.00x boot-width finding that the rendered frame reversed - name the band and check it against the frame before quoting a ratio. One finding from the same work is recorded open rather than fixed. `ActorGroundShadow2D.GROUND_FLATTEN` is 0.46 and its docstring says that is what a ground circle projects to under the bake camera's 52 degrees. Working the actual rig basis - `aaa_bake_rig.py` builds a forward vector from elevation 52 and azimuth -45 at distance 24, then tracks it - a unit ground circle projects to 1.0 horizontal by sin(52 degrees) = 0.788 vertical, and 0.46 is sin(27.4 degrees). The docstring is arithmetically false whichever value ultimately ships. It is not corrected to 0.788 here, for three reasons: the world is drawn 1:1 top-down, which argues the other way toward 1.0, so the shipping number is a judged blend of the sprite's implied projection and the map's rather than a derivation from either; the prop families share no flatten with it at all, at 0.34 on atlas contacts, 0.55 on foliage and aspect-derived on structures; and this section's own precedent requires a rendered sweep before a shadow dimension changes, while the survivors' accepted look was settled at 0.46. **A docstring that states a derivation is a claim that can be checked arithmetically**, and this one was false in the same file whose sibling constant had just been refuted for the same species of reason - a number that describes one thing being used as though it described another.

`EnemyAgent2D`, `EnemyPresentationCatalog`, and `EnemyPresentation2D` keep the eight stable simulation variant IDs while assigning one distinct scene, scale, tint family, silhouette treatment, marker language, and motion cadence to each type. All eight now read from one baked actor family under `assets/2d/actors/`: a single camera, lighting rig and palette bake each variant into eight world-space heading rows per clip, so a goliath reads as twice a walker because of its pixels rather than a per-variant scale multiplier. `ActorFacing` picks the row by dot product against measured headings, every baked scene pins its sprite to the projected world origin the bake recorded, and the catalog therefore applies one uniform `Vector2(1.0, 1.0)` scale. That constant read `Vector2(2.0, 2.0)` while the bake framed every clip on the union of all of them, so a death pose lying flat set the cell and a walker's idle resolved from about twenty real pixels which the scale then doubled. Doubling the bake density and halving the scale leaves every actor the same size on screen with real pixels behind it, and at exactly 1.0 a texel lands on a logical pixel, so the nearest filtering these sheets ask for finally means what it says. The survivors are the one exception to that uniformity and were halved by the same change: `PlayerAvatar` ships its baked actor at `BAKED_ACTOR_VISUAL_SCALE = 1.5`. Both numbers are recorded here because this paragraph carried the old one for long enough to corrupt a measurement taken against it - a cast-wide comparison of contact-shadow footprints read every enemy body at twice its shipped width, halved every enemy ratio, and produced a conclusion that reversed once the constant was read out of the code instead. **Read a scale out of the source, not out of this document; a stale constant in a contract does not announce itself, it silently corrupts whatever is measured against it.** Status presentation mirrors replicated slow/root state without adding a per-enemy light or particle budget.

`visual_validation.tscn` is the integrated art-direction benchmark and not a shipping level. Any later shader, import, or palette change should be compared against this scene before being accepted.

## 8. Visual language

The world uses deep charcoal and petroleum green as its value base, with warm corrosion and cool patina creating large environmental zones. The Base Core is the only dominant Amber Gold source, allowing it to pull visual focus even when all other effects are active. White-metal and Cyan technology occupy smaller high-value islands so resource readability never competes with the refuge at equal scale.

Raising the world's contrast put that first claim at risk, so it was re-measured rather than assumed, and the check is recorded because it failed in an instructive direction. The complaint was that the forest canopy now reads as a warm gold mass competing with the refuge. Two estimators say it does not. Warm pixels - hue 30 to 70 degrees at saturation 0.35 or above - holding more than twice the frame mean luma are 0.00 percent of the forest density capture against 0.59 percent of the refuge frame, so there is no bright warm population outside the camp at all. Surface weathering is procedural and parameterized through a single CanvasItem shader with Rust, Decayed Wood, and Oxidized Copper modes. The implementation uses bounded hash/value-noise operations without dynamic loops, which keeps instruction cost predictable on mobile. Backgrounds may use strong weathering, while interactable silhouettes should reduce `weathering` or apply clean edge masks.

The Base Core aura uses an additive radial shader and a separate `PointLight2D`. Resource and character scenes use a four-sample outline rather than a large convolution kernel, trading subpixel perfection for stable mobile cost. Godot 4 performs CanvasItem light processing in the normal draw pass, and its official [CanvasItem shader reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/canvas_item_shader.html) and [2D lights guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html) define the behavior used here.

`toon_emissive_outline.gdshader` is deliberately `blend_mix` and not `unshaded`, and the distinction is not cosmetic: an unshaded CanvasItem receives neither `CanvasModulate` nor any `PointLight2D`. Confirmed on both survivors and all three resource nodes.

An outline is a screen-space quantity - it is how heavy a contour looks to a player - so authoring it in texels means authoring it in a unit the player never sees. The offset is now `fwidth(UV) * outline_width * stretch`. `fwidth(UV)` is how far UV moves across one screen pixel, so the tap lands one screen pixel out whatever the subject's scale, for one derivative instruction and no new materials. Raw `fwidth` alone would have been wrong in a way that is easy to miss: it yields *physical* pixels, so under `canvas_items` stretch the same line would draw at a quarter weight on a 1080p panel and the offline simulation, which models logical pixels, would stop matching the render. `stretch` is `(1.0 / SCREEN_PIXEL_SIZE.x) / 480.0`, and `SCREEN_PIXEL_SIZE` is available to a canvas_item fragment shader without a screen texture being read, so section 10's single-full-screen-pass budget is untouched. The alternative was a material per quantised scale band, and it was rejected for being N things to keep correct rather than one: it re-breaks the moment any scale moves, and it cannot express a subject whose scale is chosen at runtime, which `PlacedStructure2D` does three different ways.

The fix was then confirmed on delivered pixels rather than on the algebra, because the whole point of the defect is that the authored number and the delivered number are different things. Horizontal dark-run lengths in the freshly captured structure frame give a short-run mean of 1.75 to 2.23 across the eight structures against the world props' 1.40 to 1.71 - one contour family, where before the structures carried 0.14 to 0.19 screen pixels against the world's 0.9 to 2.83. The slight excess is population rather than weight: a structure delivers into about 35 pixels, so its compressed interior line work merges with the ring, while a prop resolving into 127 to 578 pixels separates them. This is the same class as `HealthBar2D._pixel()` in section 7 - a screen-space quantity authored in a space that scales - and it is now the third, fourth and fifth instance of it. The fifth is `toon_emissive_outline.gdshader`, worn by the refuge at an effective 0.84 scale, the three resource nodes at 0.46, and both survivor scenes at 0.27, and the paragraphs below close it.

That fifth instance is now closed, and the thing that had held it back was retired by measuring it rather than by solving it. It was the better icon at 1× and it does not ship, because 1× is not where the icon is delivered. **Texture minification is a physical-pixel quantity under `canvas_items` stretch: the canvas renders at the panel's resolution, so a 1080-by-2400 phone draws this icon into 89.6 physical pixels, where the 128-pixel master is minified 1.43x at mip level 0.51 and a 24-pixel raster would be magnified 3.7x into soft blocks.** A 1× capture minifies a texture four times harder than that phone does, so a shimmer or anchor-survival finding taken at 1× is a worst-case bound rather than the shipped condition - the opposite of a contour authored through `fwidth` and `stretch`, which is a logical quantity for which 1× is exact, and the reason the two families in this section are judged at different scales. Two probe facts outlive the question. And a windowed run whose script fails to parse - here an untyped `for` iterator under `untyped_declaration = 2` - leaves the Godot window open and the command hanging rather than exiting, so it has to be killed by process before anything is re-run. `RESOURCE SCATTER VALIDATION OK (72)` and `SMOKE OK (207)` pass across the change; the two `radial_light` SVGs in `assets/2d/effects/` import without mipmaps too and are left that way on content rather than on measurement, because aliasing needs detail finer than the sample grid and a four-stop radial gradient over a 128-pixel radius has none - the texels a minified fetch skips are within a luma of the ones it reads.

The same material's grade was solved in the same sitting and against the same twenty-three bakes, because a contour weight and the value it separates are one readability problem. The complaint was that props read as flat cutouts pasted onto the terrain, and the measurement agreed in the strongest possible form: with the finish as shipped, a forest prop's body p10 sat at 55.00 against a modal forest ground of 50, and an urban prop's at 54.67 against 60. Three things about how that was picked are worth more than the values. 1.30 is the knee rather than the optimum: below it each contrast step buys 3.5 to 4.2 luma of body-to-ground separation and above it 1.0 to 1.2, and the uniform's own `hint_range` ends at 1.50, so taking the last 20 percent of the available separation would have spent all the headroom a later correction needs. The lift term is where that grid was lying, and unpicking it corrected a piece of arithmetic that had already been written into a comment twice. A body pixel whose graded luma clips to zero does not ship black: it exits at `cool_shadow * shadow_tint`, plus the lift direction at full weight - the lift is scaled by alpha and a body pixel's alpha is 1.0 - all of it lerped toward `cool_shadow` by `palette_cohesion`. Lift therefore raises the floor rather than holding anything off it, and 0.025 ships because 7.89 sits under the 8.4 this section reserves for Void charcoal outlines and cavities while 12.74 is clear of it. **A floor derived at one parameter value is not the floor at another: the lift term vanishes from the algebra at lift 0 and dominates it at lift 0.025.** The grade decision itself is unaffected, because it was taken against lift-0 rows where the two forms agree exactly - but the crush guard is not. Three findings closed the remaining routes into that change. The last criterion that did not depend on an environment mean was internal to the shader: `accent_color` reaches a pixel through a highlight term gated at `smoothstep(0.52, 0.96, luminance)` and a palette anchor driven by `smoothstep(0.16, 0.84, luminance)`, both evaluated on the ungraded source, so a body dark enough would simply not receive its identity accent and section 11's redundancy requirement would be doing the arguing rather than a taste. Two instrument notes came out of the same work and both outlive the question. The offline record grades at source resolution and then downsamples, while the GPU filters the texture first and grades the filtered texel once per screen pixel - grade-then-downsample against downsample-then-grade. At the shipped structure parameters the two agree to about 1.1 luma at p50 and 1.5 at p90, which is why the table above stands, but under a source-side gamma of 1.35 they diverge by up to 5 luma on a single subject. **A source-side operator's predicted effect is model-dependent at roughly the magnitude of one 0.05 gamma step, so an operator tuned that finely cannot be chosen on offline evidence alone.** And the delivered-size probe masks its statistics on the *composited* alpha, so its structure p10 pins at 6.2 - the ungraded outline ring's own luma of 6.16 - which is the defect recorded a few paragraphs above arriving in a third instrument of the same thread. The corrected form masks on the source alpha downsampled through the same filter as the colour, and it reproduces this section's recorded structure row exactly, which is what licensed everything measured against it.

Every CanvasItem shader under `shaders/` carried one defect that none of the paragraphs above could see, because every measurement in them was taken through it. A `canvas_item` `fragment()` enters with `COLOR` already equal to `texture(TEXTURE, UV)` multiplied by the vertex colour - the node's modulate and self-modulate - so the idiom `vec4 source = texture(TEXTURE, UV) * COLOR;` multiplies the fetch by itself, and every texel and its alpha shipped as its own per-channel square. Seven shaders had it - `sleek_sprite_finish`, `sleek_canvas_grade`, `toon_emissive_outline`, `surface_weathering`, `projectile_glow`, `solid_white_flash` and `base_scale_glow` - which between them dress every environment prop, every placed structure, the refuge, the three resource nodes, both legacy survivor scenes, the ambient and background scenery, the three weathered surface families, the projectile tracer and the hit flash. Every wearer was gamma-2.0 darkened before any grade ran. It was measured on the GPU rather than argued: a shader whose whole fragment is `COLOR = texture(TEXTURE, UV)` renders byte-identical to no material at all, and the product renders as the per-channel square. The fix is `vec4 source = COLOR;` in all seven, and each keeps the old line in its comment prose so the idiom is not reintroduced by copying a fragment header. **A shader's entry `COLOR` is already the textured, modulated fragment; a fetch multiplied back into it is a square, and a grade tuned through a square is tuned for a source the player never sees.**

Two consequences run back through the paragraphs above. Modulate now folds into entry `COLOR` once rather than twice, so a runtime tint is a plain multiply again and can only lose value - which is exactly why `WorldObstacle2D.WILD_TREE_TINT` could correct the wild canopy's hue and could not touch the contact puck section 9 records, because the puck is baked below the tint's reach. And the sprite finish's crush floor is unchanged in algebra but now sits under a source twice as bright, so the delivered minimum of a forest prop wearing the finish pins at luma 13.0 on the 12.74 closed-form floor; the body-p10-against-ground retune above was recorded at the time as decided against squared sources and owed a re-measurement rather than a re-derivation, because its guards were shaped on the right criterion; the two paragraphs below are that re-measurement, and the first thing it corrected was the attribution. The canopy-hue paragraph after the palette table was likewise measured through the squared finish and is superseded by the wild-family record in section 9.

That re-measurement is done, and its first finding is about the record rather than the render. The offline model that chose contrast 1.30 loads each atlas PNG as linear values on zero to one and never squares them, so the sweep was taken against the unsquared source the GPU only began shipping after the fix; what was wrong for the whole period was the delivered pixel and not the decision, and the sentence above attributing the decision to squared sources is corrected accordingly. The check was then made on the GPU rather than trusted from the argument. A temporary probe put all twenty-three finish wearers at their shipped scales under a 0.38 camera on their own modal grounds - 50 forest, 60 urban - with `WorldObstacle2D._get_environment_finish` read live rather than transcribed, swept the material's `contrast` over the sweep's own eight values, and wrote each subject once more with no material on a transparent viewport, so the body mask is the filtered source alpha above 0.9 and never the composite. On the twenty subjects the model knows, delivered body p10 agrees with the offline prediction to within 0.0 to 2.3 luma in the forest and 0.0 to 4.3 in the urban districts, the GPU landing slightly brighter on most, which is the grade-then-downsample against downsample-then-grade difference this section already sizes. The knee did not move. ## 9. Blender-to-Godot art pipeline

`tools/art/generate_character_sprites.py` creates a dedicated `BESPREN_SPRITE_STAGE` scene in Blender and leaves the user’s default scene intact. It builds both survivors from reproducible primitives, applies PBR materials and emission, adds three-point lighting plus Freestyle outlines, then renders 320×320 transparent PNGs into `assets/2d/characters/`. `tools/art/generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` uses the same isolated-scene policy to render the camp refuge and eight mechanically distinct structures into `assets/2d/structures/`, including the violet-triggered Razor Snare. Both scripts resolve the project root from their own location.

The render camera is orthographic and elevated at a top-down three-quarter angle. Heikki receives broader shoulders, rust tank mass, and a Gold chest slash; Shane receives a slimmer torso, asymmetrical pauldron, Cyan tech cell, and antenna. These differences deliberately survive desaturation and small-display viewing rather than relying on hue alone.

Those differences were authored and then measured, and for a long time they were not there. At the size the game actually delivers - a 320 pixel bake drawn through `Sprite.scale = 0.27` under a `zoom = 0.38` camera, so 32.8 screen pixels - Heikki and Shane were near-identical blobs separated only by hue, which is precisely the failure CLAUDE.md 11 forbids when it requires identity to be redundant across hue, silhouette, iconography, and placement. The band measurements said so plainly: Heikki led Shane in none of the six horizontal bands, trailing by 2.3 screen pixels at the head and 1.0 at the shoulders, with a centred silhouette IoU of 0.861 between two characters whose whole design premise is opposed silhouettes.

Broadening Heikki did not fix it, and the reason is the load-bearing lesson of the episode. Three successive widenings moved the shoulder band the right way and cost him symmetry every time, monotonically - mirror-IoU 0.851, 0.823, 0.806, 0.785, 0.770 - which reads as an art problem and is not one. The camera sits at `(4.25, -6.5, 7.3)` looking at `(0, 0, 1.45)`, giving camera-right `(0.837, 0.547, 0)` and camera-up `(-0.329, 0.504, 0.799)` at 68.8 pixels per world unit. Because up carries a -0.329 X component, a world-symmetric shoulder pair at plus and minus X separates in screen *Y* as well as screen X: at X 0.68 that is 0.448 units of vertical offset, so the shoulder line projected as a 21 degree diagonal. Every unit of width given to Heikki was a unit of diagonal, and "broad" and "bilaterally symmetric" were anti-correlated by the projection rather than by the art. A best-fit-axis mirror search over offsets and tilts was run first to rule out a metric artifact; it settled at 1 to 3 degrees and barely moved the numbers, because the body's near-axis mass dominates the union. The fix is `_face_camera`, which yaws the finished body by the camera's own azimuth, `atan2(-6.5, 4.25) + 90 = 33.18` degrees, mapping body +X onto camera-right and the chest onto the lens. Heikki's best-fit mirror axis then snaps to offset 0.0 and tilt 0.0 degrees, his symmetry goes to 0.998 against Shane's 0.924, and the asymmetry gap holds constant while he grows - width and asymmetry are finally independent channels rather than one budget spent twice. **The bake camera basis is authored geometry: a body built on an axis the camera does not share pays for it in projection, and the basis belongs in the record beside the model.**

The turn costs whatever was authored against the spine's back face, which is why `_FLANK_PROPS` exists: those objects are rotated back afterwards so they keep the screen position they were authored at. It recovered Shane's antenna and tech cell outright, at a full 255 delta. It did not recover Heikki's rust tank - that diff came back at max delta 4, which is bounce light and nothing else, because the occluder is `ArmR` at X 0.62 to 1.04 rather than the torso. Pairing the tanks at plus and minus 0.80 did not help either, at max delta 9 across 34 delivered pixels: present in the file, invisible on screen. Clearing both arm and pauldron laterally would need X beyond 1.45, an absurd outboard boom, so the mass moved *up* instead, to a mirrored pair at plus and minus 0.84 rising to Z 3.10 over a connecting yoke. That is worth stating as a rule rather than a fix, because the same measurement decided both halves: **the shipped tank was worth 47 delivered pixels at full opacity, so it was load-bearing and could not simply be dropped; the relocated pair is worth 2,693 bake pixels, and it lands in the head band, which was the one band still inverted.** Heikki now leads at the head by 4.6 screen pixels and at the shoulders by 3.2, the centred IoU falls from 0.861 to 0.785, and Shane is byte-identical to his pre-tank bake at max delta 0.

Making that mass visible exposed something that had been hiding behind it. `BSP_Rust` is used at exactly one site, and its 98 previously visible pixels were only ever the tank's shadow-side edge, measuring saturation 0.63; the lit material is 0.96 against the `Rust bloom #BC431A` anchor's 0.86, at hue 23.7 against the anchor's 15.2. A large mass off its own anchor is a different defect from a sliver off it, so the base colour moved from `(0.38, 0.105, 0.025)` to `(0.38, 0.089, 0.042)`, which renders at hue 18.1 and saturation 0.85. The solve is recorded because guessing at it wasted two bakes: in the red-yellow sector saturation depends only on `b/r` and hue only on `(g-b)/(r-b)`, so hitting the anchor needs blue *up* and green *down*, and raising both - the intuitive move - walks hue and saturation in the same direction and can reach neither. The regrade also corrected a metric rather than only a colour. The 20-to-70-degree band this document's separation figure calls "gold" contains rust at 23.7 degrees, so the tank had been counted as Heikki's identity accent all along; splitting the warm mass at 30 degrees, which cleanly divides the rust family from the `#FFD45A`/`#FFB52E` gold family, shows gold leading rust 2.34 to 1 at delivered scale against 3.49 to 1 before. Gold remains dominant, so CLAUDE.md 2's "Gold utility accents" holds while the rust reads as the mass CLAUDE.md 9 calls it. **A hue-band metric that straddles two anchors reports one of them as the other; check the band edges against the anchors before trusting a separation figure.**

Two procedural notes came out of the same work. A judgement made at bake resolution was wrong and the delivered-scale test reversed it: at 3x magnification the turned bodies read as flat front elevations with balloon shoulders and the shipped pose looked better, and at 33 pixels that verdict inverted completely, because the objection was about detail no player sees. This document already requires new art to be inspected at 1x logical size; the rule earns its place by having bitten in both directions now. And when a bake reports zero renders, re-run it unfiltered - a `grep` for `Saved:` hides a Python syntax error completely, which cost a cycle here.

The change is a pixel change to `heikki_topdown.png` and `shane_topdown.png`, and both are consumed by `scenes/characters/heikki.tscn`, `scenes/characters/shane.tscn`, and `scenes/ui/StartMenu.tscn` - a narrower reach than that list makes it look, and the two paragraphs after this one are about how much narrower. The official rebake matched the accepted variant at max per-channel delta 0 across all 102,400 pixels of both files, satisfying the determinism contract below. The survivor `HealthBar2D.bar_size` and `ActorGroundShadow2D.base_radius` are both flat authored values shared by the two survivors rather than alpha-derived like the Base bar, so a wider Heikki moved no gate-derived bound - and that is a note, not an endorsement: the contact shadow now spans 54 percent of his boot band against 60 percent before. That reading stood as a note for exactly as long as it took to ask what the literal behind it was anchored to; section 7 now carries the answer and the corrected radius.

Where those two PNGs actually ship is the correction this section owes, because an audit was run against them on the assumption that they are the gameplay survivors and they are not. `player_avatar.gd` says it outright - shipping defaults to the baked directional actor - so `GameWorld` draws `assets/2d/actors/scenes/hero_heikki.tscn` and reaches `scenes/characters/heikki.tscn` only through `force_legacy_presentation`, the recovery escape hatch for a malformed bake. The engine proved that before the reasoning did, and the method is worth more than the anecdote: swapping both bakes and recapturing `player_presentation_live_day.png` moved 381 pixels, every one of them in the two Heikki columns at mid-torso y 99 to 104, while Shane - whose bake had just lost 1,632 alpha pixels - came back byte identical. A byte-identical frame is a diagnosis, and what it diagnosed here was the auditor. Confirm that the shipped path draws an asset before measuring the asset.

What the audit found is real and shipped anyway. `_GroundSilhouette` was a charcoal ellipsoid of half-extents (0.92, 0.62, 0.10) built as each survivor's baked contact, and a smooth ellipsoid in a three-point rig catches the key light: it owned 2,012 pixels at 88 percent fully opaque with a mean luma of 64.5 against a whole-body mean of 75.4 and the game ground's 22.5 - two fully opaque wings flanking the boots, warm olive (79, 75, 56) on one side and cool grey (91, 104, 100) on the other. A lit primitive cannot be a shadow; a shadow is composited rather than modelled, which is the whole reason `ActorGroundShadow2D` is a sibling node and not geometry. At the delivered portrait size it read as a grey puddle wider than the boots and brighter than them, so the primitive is gone, the alpha bounds are unchanged either side of the removal, and the official rebake reproduced the accepted variant at max per-channel delta 0 across all 102,400 pixels of both files.

Nothing replaces it, and that is a measurement rather than a preference. The portrait plate composites to luma 5.7 - `Color(0.008, 0.022, 0.02, 0.92)` over the card's `Color(0.028, 0.071, 0.064, 0.97)` - so a contact shadow drawn there would have under six luma of headroom to darken into, against the 22.5 ground where `GroundShadow` has real range. Two per-asset knobs in `generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` are worth stating because one was kept and one was tried, measured, and thrown away. The character generator has now been through that same argument and come out the other way, which is only defensible because the measurement was different. Rendering the survivors at that true size instead surfaced the defect worth fixing, and it is hue rather than brightness. That is the same knob the camp script had already tried and deleted, so the difference has to be stated rather than assumed. That gap is now closed. `write_generated_asset_manifest.py` used to name `generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` as its single generator and cover exactly the nine camp and structure renders, so the character family - the other half of what `tools/art/` bakes - carried no provenance record at all, which is the worse form of the failure its own docstring warns about: not a manifest gone stale but a manifest never written, against which `smoke_test.gd` could only assert that the paths exist. The camp does keep its own warm firelight, and its isolation is structural rather than a per-light toggle. The render loop hides every asset collection except the one being rendered, and a light parented inside a hidden collection contributes nothing - so the camp's rig cannot reach the other eight renders, and no separate light bookkeeping has to stay correct for that to hold.

Two properties of the AgX Medium High Contrast view transform shape how these bakes are judged. It desaturates monotonically with brightness, so removing a large fill light reads as both darker and *more* saturated - a change that looks like two edits when it is one, and which has misled a bake review here before. Consequently a bake is compared against the previous PNG rather than against an expectation of what removing a light should do. `tools/art/write_generated_asset_manifest.py` regenerates the manifest for this whole generated family and must be re-run after any rebake, since nothing else notices that a PNG changed.

That manifest used to record "EEVEE is not bit deterministic", and the correction is a contract rather than a footnote. Three consecutive bakes of an unmodified generator do produce three different file digests - but at an identical byte count, and decoding them shows byte-identical IDAT streams with a max per-channel delta of exactly 0 across all 102,400 pixels. Only the `tEXt` `Date` and `RenderTime` chunks differ, because Blender stamps the wall clock and the measured render duration outside the pixel data. The old wording licensed the wrong conclusion - it made a rebake whose pixels had genuinely drifted read as expected noise. A rebake of unmodified source must match the shipped pixels exactly, and any non-zero delta is a regression to explain rather than absorb. The recorded per-file `sha256` therefore identifies the file that shipped rather than one a rerun reproduces byte for byte, and `generator_sha256` pins the source.

That `ground_offset` is faithful to its own definition and was still the wrong number for one job. The village fences carried a repetition defect the atlas could not have caused, and the first fix for it looked right and was not. Each of the twelve village fences - four horizontal 1500-unit runs and two vertical 1700-unit runs per village - is drawn by `_install_local_fence_visuals` as four segments cut from `LOCAL_WOOD_BARRICADE_REGION`, and every segment shipped the same frame at the same orientation, so a fence read as one stamp repeated four times at equal gaps: on the west village capture, three segment runs 33 to 34 pixels wide at gaps of 19, 21 and 20. The frame is strongly asymmetric - mirror IoU 0.48 over its own alpha, mean absolute luma difference against its own mirror 41.08 - so a horizontal flip gives a segment a genuinely different silhouette at no atlas cost, and at the village capture scale a segment's self-mirror MAD is 20 to 21.5 luma against 5 to 9 between two same-orientation neighbours. That pair of numbers is the estimator the whole change is judged on, because it is what caught the first fix. That fix flipped each segment on an independent seeded coin, `_unit_noise(noise_base) < 0.5`, and a per-segment coin leaves all four segments identical on one fence in eight and three identical neighbours on half of them; a probe over the twelve fences found exactly that - `WestVillageFence_03` at 0,0,0,0 and `EastVillageFence_02` at 1,1,1,1 - and the 1x mirror test on the visible west fence returned direct MAD 5.3, 6.6 and 9.4 against mirror 20.7 to 21.5 on every pair, which is three same-orientation segments. What had read in the capture as distinct orientations was the slip and wobble cadence. **A per-segment coin flip does not deliver adjacent variety; it delivers a distribution over runs of the same thing, and the run the flip exists to remove is the likeliest one.** What ships is one seeded phase per fence, `fence_flip_phase = _unit_noise(FENCE_VARIATION_NOISE_BASE) < 0.5`, with `flip_h = fence_flip_phase != (segment_index % 2 == 1)`, so every adjacent plain pair inside a fence differs by construction and two fences with the same count still differ by phase. Beside the flip, a bounded along-axis slip of plus or minus `FENCE_SEGMENT_SLIP_FACTOR` 0.05 of a span from `_unit_noise(noise_base + 1)` and a uniform size wobble of plus or minus `FENCE_SEGMENT_SIZE_WOBBLE` 0.05 from `_unit_noise(noise_base + 2)` break the equal-gap cadence, with `noise_base = FENCE_VARIATION_NOISE_BASE + segment_index * 3`; two neighbours slipping toward each other close by at most 0.10 of a span and a segment is 0.76 of one wide, so they cannot touch. ## 10. Mobile rendering and performance policy

The project selects Godot’s `mobile` rendering method and Vulkan on Android, with OpenGL fallback enabled for unsupported devices. Godot’s Mobile renderer is designed around mobile GPU bandwidth and tile-based rendering, while HDR 2D would switch the viewport to higher-bandwidth RGBA16F; this foundation therefore leaves HDR 2D disabled. Emission is produced through controlled CanvasItem color and local lights rather than a screen-wide glow post-process.

The official [Godot 4.7 rendering architecture](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html) documents the Mobile renderer’s lower bandwidth path and the cost of extra full-screen passes. The [ProjectSettings reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_projectsettings.html) identifies `mobile` as the modern lower-overhead renderer for mobile devices. These sources justify the current renderer, single weather overlay, disabled HDR 2D, and lack of desktop post-processing.

| Budget | Foundation target | Escalation rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Logical resolution | 480×270 | Change only with UI, readability, and thermal evidence |
| Frame target | 60 FPS preferred, 30 FPS fallback | Measure on representative Android hardware |
| Frame time | 16.67 ms preferred | Quality tier may reduce weather/light energy at sustained misses |
| Full-screen custom passes | 1 | Combine atmosphere in the existing weather shader |
| Shadow-casting 2D lights | 0 in foundation | Add only after profiling and readability proof |
| Dynamic shader loops | 0 | Use bounded math or authored lookup textures |
| Local players | 2 | More seats require a new camera/input/UI contract |

The current showcase intentionally stresses several local lights at once for visual verification. Shipping scenes should use visibility culling, turn off presentation processing when off-screen, and limit overlapping lights on the same canvas region. Optimization must respond to profiler and device evidence, not to arbitrary code-size or asset-count targets.

## 16. Tactical world and salvage slice

`BesprenWorldMap2D` defines a centered 14×14 macro-grid with 2,048-unit cells and exact ±14,336 playable extents. Kaupunki occupies the northwest blocks, Ostari forms a collapsed commercial choke in the north-east interior, two separate Kylät occupy the south, and Metsä/Luonto wraps the perimeter and wilderness belts. The asphalt cross, district branches, dirt village paths, and forest perimeter route form one connected visual road graph.

The rendering matrix is absolute: ground `TileMapLayer`s and roads use `z_index = -20`, non-colliding rubble and moss use `-5`, and gameplay/static occlusion participants use `5` beneath explicit Y-sort containers. Every generated building shell, mall wall, wreck, house, fence, tree trunk, rock, and world edge owns a `CollisionShape2D` on `WorldStatic`. The same obstacle objects bake a 112×112 occupancy mask and resolve peer-one movement before authoritative positions are broadcast.

`BesprenResourceScatter2D` produces 87 stable resource IDs from a fixed seed: three Base Core teaching anchors and 84 stratified global sectors. Spawn rejection uses playable bounds, canonical obstacle footprints, clearance, and minimum spacing. Each stable ID derives a deterministic requirement of three, four, or five accepted interactions. Clients submit only the existing `interact` command; peer one uses its stored authoritative player position and per-peer focus lock, advances one reliable ordered revision, grants zero on intermediate steps, and grants the same single unit only when the node completes. Late-join snapshots include availability and partial progress for every node. See `docs/WORLD_MAP_FOUNDATION.md` for the coordinate, layering, network, and validation contracts.

`is_position_walkable` is answered through a 512-unit obstacle broadphase grid rather than a linear scan. It sits on the authoritative path - `resolve_player_motion` makes up to three calls per player per 20 Hz tick - so with two seats the scan over all 461 obstacles cost roughly 25 ms of every wall-clock second. Obstacles stamp their zero-clearance world bounds into the grid, but a rotated rectangle expands along its own local axes, so a query pads by `clearance * sqrt(2)` to stay a strict superset of what the exact test could accept. Measured over 6,000 queries the scan runs at 209.9 us and the grid at 3.8 us, a 55.2x reduction with 0 mismatches against a brute-force reference. The sampling is the load-bearing part of that proof: half the points were drawn inside an obstacle's own padded bounds, giving 34.1 percent actually blocked, because uniform sampling of a 28,672-unit world puts almost every point where the pad cannot be wrong and would let a broken grid score a clean run.

The ground-cover family is the ecology layer beneath that: 3,245 clusters and 38,947 elements, batched at two draw commands per cluster across 170 render chunks, gated by biome, walkability, road verge, and the camp clearance, and drawn with no material at all. Materiallessness was checked rather than assumed - it is not the `render_mode unshaded` opt-out that exempts a shader subject from the night `CanvasModulate`, and measured across the day and night captures the blade tips darken at ratio 0.631 against the ground's 0.630 and the frame's 0.633. Its density was also nearly retuned on a stale complaint. The observation that only about five elements read per screen was true of the first colour pass and false afterwards; the fix had been to author each kind backwards from the measured floor so it presents a value pair rather than a tint, and the current gameplay capture carries 4.42 percent of its pixels at least 18 luma above the modal ground and 7.10 percent at least 8 below it across 2,761 lit runs. Tripling the cluster count would have multiplied something that was never missing, so the constants stand and the placement cost stays at 192.5 ms of world build, down from 1,908 ms before the broadphase.

No destructive cleanup of `Addons/` is authorized by this document. If asset-vault reduction becomes necessary, first build a license and dependency manifest, separate safe generated caches from human-authored sources, and request confirmation before any irreversible deletion. This keeps the current foundation reproducible while respecting the project’s source material.
